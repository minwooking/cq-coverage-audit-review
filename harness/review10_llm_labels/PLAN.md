## 30.1 Status, question, claim ceiling

Exploratory and post hoc. No confirmatory hypothesis. The thresholds in section 30.8 only decide which sentence the result supports.

**Question.** Several role-played LLM raters, each sampled several times, produce an evidence label `S` in {0, 1} for a (question, rendering) cell: does a verbatim span of the rendering support an answer to the question? A cell is kept only if the labels it receives are consistent under a rule fixed before any comparison with humans. How well do the kept (consistent) labels agree with the two human raters, compared with how the two human raters agree with each other **on the same cells**?

**Claim ceiling (fixed before any run).** The human labels stay the reference. This is not a replacement of the human labels, and agreement with a human is an agreement number, not accuracy. A result of the form "close to human on the consistent cells" would say something about a self-selected part of the cells (the coverage is reported next to it) and nothing about the cells where the labeler is not self-consistent. No sentence of this addendum is to be read as "LLM labels are more accurate than human labels".

**What this session knew before the runs (disclosure).** The addendum 28 results (`harness/review6_raters/REPORT.md`): GLM-4.7-Flash and gpt-oss-20b with the S21 prompt at one temperature or another had kappa with the two human raters between -0.06 and 0.16 on S (human-human 0.595 on 188 cells), Krippendorff alpha 0.40 across temperature 0.7 repeats and paraphrases, and marked 19 to 36% of cells supported against 67% and 73% for the humans. The registered holdout judge's scores and the unsupported-credit rates on the 188 cells (7.4% on the AH labels, 13.8% on the second rater's labels). **No output of any persona prompt of this addendum has been seen**, and none of these prompts has been sent to any model. The expectation that follows from the earlier runs (a low share of consistent cells and low agreement) is a prior, not a hypothesis; nothing below is tuned toward or away from it.

**Seeds.** 5 per persona: seed parameter 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Temperature 0.7, top-p and every other sampling parameter at the server default. Maximum output tokens as in addendum 28, per model (section 30.4). That is **25 labels per cell per model** (5 personas x 5 seeds). If an endpoint does not forward or accepts no `seed`, it is omitted and the five repeats are unseeded draws labelled 1..5 by call index; this is recorded in the run metadata.

**Label.** The model's JSON reply is parsed by `rater_luna.parse_content` (strict: one JSON object, `S` in {0,1}, `R` in {0..3}). `S_raw` is the model's `S`. **`S_final`** (primary label) is `S_raw` after the deterministic span check of addendum 28: `S_final = 1` only if `S_raw = 1` and the cited `span`, whitespace-normalised, occurs in the whitespace-normalised rendering the model saw; otherwise 0 (the same rule as the humans' protocol, "a verbatim span of the rendering supports the answer"). `S_raw` is a reported sensitivity, never chosen after seeing agreement. An unparsable reply is retried at most twice with the larger token budget; a call that stays unparsable is `invalid` and its slot has no label.

## 30.5 Cells and tiers

**Tier 1 (analysed).** The 188 primary holdout cells that have both human labels (33 question clusters), in the material of addendum 28: the question-relevant subgraph view for 182 cells and the full rendering for the 6 cells without a subgraph view (`A:CQB27`, `B..F:CQB32`). The 65,536-token window of `glm` and `gptoss` cannot hold those 6 prompts (A:CQB27 about 74k tokens, the others 0.5-0.6M), so for those two models the 6 cells are logged as `context_skip` and are not evaluated; `qwen` (window 262k) is sent A:CQB27 and logs the five B..F:CQB32 cells as `context_skip`. The evaluated set of a model is therefore at most 182 cells (`glm`, `gptoss`) and 183 cells (`qwen`).

**Order.** Cells are processed in the order `random.Random(20260913).shuffle(sorted cell ids)`, the same for all models, cells are dispatched in that order with at most three in flight, and the five seeds of one (cell, persona) pair are sent one after the other by one worker (so the server's prefix cache helps). A model's evaluated cells are the cells for which all 25 slots were attempted. If a run stops early (stop rule, wall-clock cap, time), the evaluated cells are those it completed, and because the order is a seeded shuffle that does not use any label, they are a random subset of the cells; coverage and agreement are then reported on that subset and the shortfall is stated.

**Tier 2 (coverage of the consistency filter only; no human labels).** Run only after Tier 1 of the primary models is complete and only if time allows: (a) the 28 (variant, question) cells of the 216-cell grid of the length experiment (6 variants x 36 questions) that are **not** among the 188 primary cells (subgraph views of `harness/length_experiment/inputs/manifest.jsonl`, sha256 checked); (b) the 72 natural-documentation A/B cells of addendum 27 (subgraph views of `harness/review6_natural/inputs/manifest.jsonl`, sha256 checked). Questions outside the 33 primary clusters have no site translation; the Korean category is taken from the same category in the primary manifest and the translation line is omitted. For Tier 2 only the coverage of the consistency filter (shares under the three rules, S=1 share of the consistent labels, S=1 share of the unfiltered majority) is reported, with a question-cluster BCa interval; **no agreement number is computed, because there is no human label.**

**Pilot (not analysed).** Before Tier 1, 10 calls per available model are sent for timing and a parse check: the first two cells of the Tier-1 order, the five personas, seed 1. They are written under `pilot/`, are excluded from every analysis, and the same slots are sent again inside Tier 1. The pilot sets the wall-clock schedule (`SCHEDULE.md`, committed before Tier 1) but changes no rule, prompt, persona, cell or threshold of this file.

## 30.6 Consistency rules (fixed here)

For model `m` and cell `i`, let `c1` and `c0` be the numbers of the 25 slots whose `S_final` is 1 and 0. A slot that is invalid or missing counts for neither (it never agrees with anything).

* **R25 (primary).** The model's label of the cell is **consistent** if `max(c0, c1) >= 23` (at least 23 of the 25 labels agree, 92%). The consistent label is the majority label. 
* **R25U (secondary).** Consistent if `max(c0, c1) = 25` (unanimous).
* **RX (secondary, cross-model consensus).** A cell has a consensus label if at least 2 models have an R25-consistent label on it and all models that have one give the same label. Two variants, both reported: **RX-primary** (models `glm` and `gptoss` only, so both must be consistent and agree) and **RX-all** (the three models; at least 2 of them consistent, all consistent ones agreeing).

The secondary rules are reported next to the primary rule and are not selected after seeing human agreement. Coverage = consistent cells / evaluated cells of the model (for RX: cells with a label among the cells where at least two of the contributing models were evaluated).

## 30.7 Metrics (all on `S_final` unless a `S_raw` sensitivity is named)

Human sources: `AH` (the first human rater) and `R2` (the second rater), the `S` labels of the two analysis files. All intervals are question(CQ)-clustered BCa, 95%, B = 10,000, seed 20260913, the analyzer's resampling (`common.fast_bca`; checked against `analyze_holdout.cluster_bootstrap_stat` in the self-test, as in `review9_verbatim`). Cohen's kappa is the binary kappa of `analyze_raters.kappa_bin`; a degenerate chance term gives 0.

1. **Coverage** of each rule, per model and for RX, with an interval; the split of the consistent cells by variant (A..F) and the share of consistent cells that carry label 1.
2. **Agreement of the consistent labels with each human.** On the consistent subset `C` of (model, rule): kappa and exact agreement of the consistent LLM label with AH, and with R2.
3. **Human-human agreement on the same cells.** On `C`: kappa and exact agreement of AH with R2; and on the complement `E \ C` (the evaluated cells that are not consistent): the same. Paired difference `Delta_h = kappa(LLM-consistent, h) - kappa(AH, R2)` on `C`, with an interval.
4. **Unsupported credit `pi-hat`** (the paper's definition: the share of cells with registered-judge score `J >= 2` and `S = 0`, denominator all cells of the set) on `C`, computed (i) under the consistent LLM label, (ii) under AH, (iii) under R2, all on the same cells; with the paired differences `pi-hat(LLM) - pi-hat(h)` and intervals. (The paper's values on all 188 cells are 7.4% for AH and 13.8% for R2.)
5. **Persona effects.** For each (model, persona) the persona label is the majority of its 5 seed labels (a tie among an even number of valid labels leaves the cell without a label for that persona): the share of cells with `S = 1`, kappa with AH and with R2 on the model's evaluated cells, and `pi-hat`. Descriptive spread: max minus min across the five personas of each of these, and the mean pairwise kappa between the five persona labels. **Unfiltered reference:** the majority of all valid labels of the 25 (ties undefined) on all evaluated cells, kappa with AH and with R2, and its `pi-hat`; this is the labeler without the consistency filter.
6. **Test-retest.** Krippendorff alpha (nominal, binary) across the 5 seeds, per (model, persona); alpha across all 25 slots (cells x 25 coders) per model; alpha between the five persona labels. Alpha is additive over cells (`o_off`, `N1`, `N0`), so it has the same cluster BCa.

Sensitivities (labelled as such, none selects anything): `S_raw` instead of `S_final`; the labels of 3 personas or 3 seeds only are **not** computed (that would be a post hoc choice of rule).

## 30.8 Interpretation thresholds (fixed now)

For the primary models (`glm`, `gptoss`), rule R25, and each human `h` in {AH, R2}: let `LB` be the **lower bound of the 95% interval of kappa(LLM-consistent label, h)** on the model's consistent subset `C`, and let `ref = kappa(AH, R2) on the same cells C (point estimate) - 0.10`. The comparison **(model, h) is close to human iff `|C| >= 30` and `LB >= ref`.** A subset with fewer than 30 cells is "not evaluable" and counts as not close.

* **Close to human:** all four (model, human) comparisons are close.
* **Mixed:** at least one but not all four are close.
* **Otherwise** (none is close): **the result supports the paper's current conclusion** (LLM-rater labels remain a sensitivity analysis, not a substitute for the human labels).

The secondary model `qwen`, the rules R25U and RX, the `S_raw` sensitivity and the persona and unfiltered references are reported, not counted. Even under "close to human", the reading is bounded by section 30.1: it concerns the consistent subset only and says nothing about accuracy.

## 30.9 Operations, stop rules, and what is recorded

* **Resumable.** One JSONL record is appended per call: cell, persona, seed, model alias, `S_raw`, `R_raw`, quoted span, note (truncated to 400 characters), `S_final`, verbatim flag, parse status, finish reason, token counts and latency; no view text and no host name, address, port or key. A re-invocation sends only slots without a valid record.
* **Stop rules** (as in addendum 28): HTTP 402 or 403, an HTTP 429 whose Retry-After exceeds 300 s, or 5 consecutive failed calls stop that model's run, with no retry loop; context-length replies are recorded as `context_skip`. The `qwen` route carries a token budget and a wall-clock cap set in `SCHEDULE.md`.
* **Progress.** `PROGRESS.md` gets one line per tier or model completion (calls done / planned, parse rate, ETA).
* **Deviations.** Any change after this commit (a bug fix, an operational change) is recorded in `DEVIATIONS.md` with the reason. No persona, rule, threshold, prompt, cell set or statistic is changed after the first Tier-1 call; if one had to be, it needs a new addendum.
* **Analysis order.** `analyze.py` and its tests are written and committed before the real comparison with the human labels is first run. The tests use synthetic data only.

## 30.11 What the addendum does not claim

It does not claim that an LLM label is accurate, that the consistency filter selects correct labels, or that the labeler can replace human raters. It measures how often a persona ensemble is self-consistent on this task and, where it is, how its labels agree with the two humans compared with how the humans agree with each other on the same cells. A positive result would show agreement on a subset; a negative result is the expected one under the paper's current conclusion and is reported as it comes out.
